6.1 What Agents Actually Do for a Small Business

Concrete use cases, not the vague idea of "AI automation."

Before you start this lesson

  • Your client pipeline app from 3.3
  • One real client, or one real workflow you have watched up close

"Agent" gets used loosely. For this course, an agent is a small piece of automation that watches for something happening (a new lead, a missed call, a form submission) and takes a specific action without you doing it by hand.

Three concrete examples

  • Lead qualification: a new lead comes into your client's pipeline (built in Module 3) → the agent sends them a text or email asking 2-3 qualifying questions → updates their status automatically based on the answers.
  • Follow-up nudges: a lead sits untouched for 3 days → the agent sends a check-in message on the business's behalf, so no lead goes cold from someone forgetting to follow up.
  • Booking confirmation: a booking comes in → the agent confirms it, sends a reminder 24 hours out, reducing no-shows without anyone lifting a finger.

Notice none of these are impressive-sounding "AI" — they're boring, reliable, and they directly stop the exact money-loss you identified back in 1.2.

What an agent means here specifically

Forget the standalone chatbot. In this context an agent is something that sits on top of an application you already built, with access to the data already in it, doing a job the owner currently does by hand.

That distinction matters commercially. A chatbot bolted onto a website is a commodity, and it's being sold by everyone right now at falling prices. An agent that reads the actual booking records in the system you built for them, and acts on them, isn't something a competitor can drop in — because they don't have the application underneath.

The kinds of jobs that work well:

  • Triage. Sorting incoming enquiries by urgency or type, so the owner opens the right one first.
  • Follow-up. Noticing a quote that went quiet for five days and drafting the nudge.
  • Summarising. Turning a week of records into the three things the owner actually needs to know on Monday.
  • Data entry. Taking messy input — an email, a voicemail transcript — and putting it in the right place in the system.

Every one of those depends on the app existing first. This is why Module 3 comes before Module 6, and why the agent work is the most defensible thing you sell.
